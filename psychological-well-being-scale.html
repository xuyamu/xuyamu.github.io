Psychological Well-Being Scale

18-item version of Ryff’s Scales of Psychological Well-Being

About this scale

This questionnaire is the 18-item short form of Carol Ryff’s Scales of Psychological Well-Being. It assesses eudaimonic well-being — how fully a person is functioning — rather than momentary happiness or life satisfaction alone. Respondents rate how much they agree with each statement. Higher scores indicate stronger psychological well-being.

The scale covers six theoretically distinct dimensions, each measured by three items (score range 3–21):

  • Autonomy — self-determination and the ability to resist social pressure.
  • Environmental Mastery — competence in managing everyday life and surroundings.
  • Personal Growth — continued development, openness, and realizing one’s potential.
  • Positive Relations with Others — warm, trusting, and reciprocal relationships.
  • Purpose in Life — goals, direction, and a sense that life is meaningful.
  • Self-Acceptance — a positive attitude toward oneself, including past life.

The total score ranges from 18 to 126. Positively worded items are reverse-scored so that a higher score always reflects greater well-being. Ryff has not published official norms or clinical cut-offs. The High / Moderate / Low labels in this report are a simple descriptive split by item average, not a standardized classification.

This tool is for education, self-reflection, and screening support only. It is not a diagnosis and does not replace assessment by a qualified professional.

Instructions: Select one response below each statement to indicate how much you agree or disagree. Please answer all 18 questions. There are no right or wrong answers; respond according to how you generally feel.
