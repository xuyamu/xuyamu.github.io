I initially took 200mg of Sodium Valproate and Ergothioneine, but found they weren’t enough to rescue my mood. I then added 400mg of Pirfenidone, 250mg of UDCA, Vitamin C, and Glutathione, and my mood finally returned to normal(But it was far from pleasant.). In the article, I will discuss why anti-aging drugs are capable of improving one’s emotional state.

　　Symptoms of this mental breakdown included persistent negative emotions, a sense of having lost interest in everything, and a feeling that there was no hope for the future; it was characterized by panic and despair—an overwhelming confluence of all negative emotions.

　　The reasons for my breakdown were:

　　1. My concern regarding the widespread negative psychological impact of AI technology on humanity.
　　2. The fact that I have the ability to help those suffering from “Artificial Intelligence Replacement Dysfunction,” yet I am unable to reach them.

　　However, most people suffering from Artificial Intelligence Replacement Dysfunction are not biohackers like me; they lack the capacity to rescue themselves from a mental breakdown. Thinking of their plight is truly heart-wrenching. My mood will gradually recover once I take some more medication.

I suspect that low doses of both wouldn't be enough to withstand this level of anxiety, while high doses would simply knock you out. Whether the anti-inflammatory effects of sodium valproate and lithium carbonate could replace pirfenidone is hard to say—the pathways are different, after all—but taking both simultaneously would definitely require aggressive liver and kidney protection.

　　Neural mechanisms of acute stress reaction:
　　During an acute stress reaction, the following phenomena occur:

　　1.Amygdala hyperactivation; inhibition of prefrontal function[1]locus coeruleus hyperarousal (the locus coeruleus is the primary source of norepinephrine in the brain) [2].

　　2. Massive production of epinephrine and norepinephrine[3].

　　3. Activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, leading to a massive release of cortisol[3].

　　4. Acute stress triggers a massive release of the excitatory neurotransmitter glutamate into the synaptic cleft, inducing excitotoxicity (a heavy influx of calcium ions into cells causes mitochondria to absorb large amounts of calcium, generating abundant reactive oxygen species [ROS] and triggering oxidative stress; excessive cytosolic calcium further causes calcium depletion from the endoplasmic reticulum, resulting in endoplasmic reticulum [ER] stress)[4].

　　5. Acute stress can reduce GABAergic inhibitory function in certain brain regions (especially the prefrontal cortex), shifting the excitation/inhibition balance toward excitation[5].

　　6. In certain high-intensity stress models, a transient elevation of pro-inflammatory cytokines (primarily IL-1β) can be observed in the brain (especially the hippocampus) [6]; this neuroinflammatory response may further exacerbate excitotoxicity and contribute to subsequent neuronal damage processes.

　　Research indicates that in rat models of excitotoxicity-induced motoneuron injury, stress exacerbates neuronal loss and microglial proliferation, thereby increasing the risk of spinal neuron damage caused by excitotoxicity or neuroinflammation[7].
Therefore, after this acute stress reaction occurred, I promptly took sodium valproate to increase GABA levels and reduce inflammation (the anti-inflammatory effect of sodium valproate is primarily due to its role as an HDAC inhibitor). I then used UDCA (ursodeoxycholic acid) to reduce inflammation and ER stress. Pirfenidone served as a potent anti-inflammatory agent, while ergothioneine, vitamin C, and glutathione acted as antioxidants.

　　An acute stress reaction typically takes several days to subside, and symptoms may persist even longer. If symptoms last for a month, it evolves into post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).

　　However, my symptoms began to ease within an hour of taking the medications, and I essentially recovered within half a day.
In older individuals, an acute stress reaction episode may lead to death due to stress-induced cardiomyopathy (Takotsubo cardiomyopathy, also known as "broken-heart syndrome").
The death of Vincent van Gogh's younger brother, Theo van Gogh, was related to immune decline caused by immense psychological stress. Vincent's death was a devastating blow to Theo. Shortly after Vincent passed away, Theo's mental state deteriorated, and he died in an asylum six months later. Theo had previously contracted syphilis; psychological stress compromised his immune system, causing the infection to flare up and rapidly take his life.

　　Emperor Hui of Han (汉惠帝), Liu Ying (刘盈), suffered a massive psychological trauma due to his mother Lü Zhi’s (吕雉) murder of Liu Ruyi (刘如意) (the son of Lady Qi (戚夫人)) and her brutal persecution of Lady Qi (戚夫人). He was completely broken by these events and passed away at the young age of 23. The Records of the Grand Historian: Annals of Empress Lü (史记·吕太后本纪) records:
　　"When Emperor Hui saw [the victim] and inquired, he realized it was Lady Qi. He wept bitterly and fell ill, remaining bedridden for over a year. He sent a messenger to the Empress Dowager, saying: 'This is not the act of a human being. As your son, I will never be able to rule the empire.' From then on, the Emperor gave himself over to daily drinking and carnal pleasures, neglecting his administrative duties, which contributed to his persistent illness."( 孝惠见，问，乃知其戚夫人，乃大哭，因病，岁馀不能起。使人请太后曰：“此非人所为。臣为太后子，终不能治天下。”孝惠以此日饮为淫乐，不听政，故有病也。)

　　From the perspective of modern psychology and medicine, Emperor Hui’s symptoms, behavioral changes, and ultimate fate align with the characteristics of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) and Major Depressive Disorder (MDD).
